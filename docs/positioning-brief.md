### For the landing-site restructure (homepage reframe + Hotel vertical subpage)

**Date:** 2026-08-01 · **Status:** research synthesis, pending founder direction on the open decisions (§9)
**Purpose:** Move the site from *"a CRM/chatbot with AI features"* to *"a revenue-generating teammate you hire"* — and stand up a deep Hotel subpage — grounded in the state of the art on AI-SaaS positioning, value-based pricing, conversion psychology, and a competitor teardown.

This brief is the reference for every copy and structure decision downstream. It's meant to be edited as we iterate.

## 0. TL;DR

**The reframe:** Stop selling *software you use*. Start selling *a worker you hire*. A tool competes for the small software budget an owner tries to **minimize** (benchmarked against $10–50/mo chatbots). An employee competes for the **headcount budget**, anchored to a salary — a line the owner *expects* to be large and judges on ROI, not price. This single shift is what unlocks both a bolder category and a higher, defensible price.

**The one-line positioning (recommended):**
> **conagentes es el vendedor con IA que llena tu hotel.** Reserva directo, sube el valor de cada estadía y recupera huéspedes — por WhatsApp, 24/7. Tú apruebas; se paga solo.

**The three things no competitor can say at once (our wedge):** WhatsApp-first direct-booking **+** a *free built-in PMS* (they're all bolt-ons) **+** DIAN e-invoicing & SIRE compliance (not one competitor mentions it).

## 1. The strategic reframe: from tool → worker (and why it protects margin)

The founder's instinct is backed by the category's defining investors and pricing analysts:

- **a16z — "Software is Eating Labor" (Alex Rampell, Oct 2025):** the market for software that *does the work* isn't the ~**$300B** software market — it's the ~**$13T** US labor market (~40× larger). *"The wages of nurses alone are more than twice the revenue of every SaaS company."* → [a16z](https://a16z.com/podcast/software-is-eating-labor/)
- **Foundation Capital — "Service-as-Software" ($4.6T):** *"software becomes the worker itself"* and, crucially for margin, when an agent completes a whole job it *"can be categorized as a **personnel cost, not a software expense**. This opens up a vastly bigger market opportunity."* → [Foundation Capital](https://foundationcapital.com/ideas/a-system-of-agents-brings-service-as-software-to-life)
- **Salesforce — "digital labor" (the incumbent's air cover):** the SaaS pioneer itself now sells agents as *"a digital workforce… that augments your human workforce,"* explicitly *"a business model"* like SaaS — *"they don't just assist — they act."* Benioff: *"When we're talking about agents, we're talking about digital labor."* The category we're entering is investor- **and** incumbent-endorsed, not a gimmick. → [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/agentforce/digital-labor/)
- **Bessemer — AI Pricing Playbook:** *"**Soft ROI positioning kills willingness-to-pay.**"* Cost-saving/efficiency framing caps your price at the salary you replace; **revenue ("hard ROI") framing** commands far more. *"AI is more akin to adding co-workers, not just tools."* → [Bessemer](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-ai-pricing-and-monetization-playbook)
- **Kyle Poyar (Growth Unhinged):** per-agent/outcome pricing *"accesses headcount budget (**10× larger** than tech tools budget)."* → [Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/ai-agent-pricing-framework)
- **Vertical premium:** vertical SaaS commands **2–3× higher ACV** than horizontal "for SMBs" tools (OpenView). "For hotels" out-earns "for any business." → [Monetizely](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/vertical-specific-saas-pricing-why-industry-context-matters-for-revenue-growth)

**Implication:** every headline leads with the *business outcome* (rooms filled, guests recovered, revenue) **before** the word "IA." Leading with "AI-powered" trips the skeptic's filter; leading with the outcome anchors value to a salary, not to competing chatbots.

## 2. Positioning statement (April Dunford framework)

- **Competitive alternative (what they'd do without us):** *not another vendor* — it's **the status quo**: front-desk staff answering WhatsApp by hand, at night nobody answers, messages missed, bookings lost to Booking.com. This is the target of our loss-aversion copy.
- **Unique attributes (with proof):** proactive selling (upsell/reactivation/lead-qualification/booking, not just Q&A) · **free built-in PMS** (rivals are bolt-ons) · **DIAN + SIRE compliance** (uncontested) · WhatsApp/Instagram + **voice** · es-CO, made-in-Colombia · "show-the-work" transparency (ApprovalCard grammar).
- **Value themes:** (1) *más ingresos* (reservas directas, upsell, reactivación); (2) *cero fricción / cero contratar más gente*; (3) *tranquilidad y control* (cumples la ley, tú apruebas, un humano toma el control).
- **Best-fit customer (launch):** independent & boutique Colombian hotels, ~**6–40 rooms**, ADR $80k–$800k COP, already living on WhatsApp, understaffed at night — a team that physically **cannot** give every guest instant, personalized, follow-up selling, so reservations slip and tickets stay low.
- **Market frame of reference (what kind of thing we are):** **"un vendedor con IA para tu hotel"** — a role you hire, reinforced by the familiar anchor *"tu WhatsApp, pero que vende por ti 24/7."*

---

> **Core problem framing — REFRAMED (2026-08-02, Sebastián).** The villain is **NOT the OTAs**. It's that a **human team physically cannot give every guest instant, personalized, follow-up, upselling attention 24/7** — guests write at all hours, in many languages, wanting an instant answer + a good recommendation. You either keep hiring, or you eat **reservas perdidas + tickets más bajos**. The AI seller is the seller that *can* be everywhere — so you capture what your team can't, **without growing it**. OTAs are a channel we *unify* (see the integrations story), never the enemy. Do NOT run "deje de regalarle su margen a las OTAs / commission-theft" copy; lead with the bandwidth gap and its two costs.

## 3. Message architecture

### 3.1 Category frame — pick ONE to own (others become supporting messages)

| Frame | Line | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **A. AI seller you hire** *(recommended primary)* | «Un vendedor con IA que trabaja 24/7» | Escapes the "chatbot que responde" bucket; anchors price to a comercial's salary. |
| **B. Commercial front desk** *(hotel wedge)* | «Recepción comercial 24/7: la que vende, no solo responde» | Reuses the owner's existing "recepción" budget slot; beats HiJiffy/Asksuite at their own "front desk" game by making it *commercial*. |
| **C. Digital employee** *(umbrella/vision)* | «Un empleado digital que genera ingresos» | Purest Service-as-Software; best for the "por qué somos distintos" section + multi-vertical expansion. |
| **D. Outcome/risk-reversal** *(proof, not hero)* | «Pagas por reservas, no por software» · «Se paga solo» | Sierra's incentive-alignment logic; the strongest trust cue for a buyer burned by chatbots. |

**Do NOT use:** "chatbot," "asistente virtual," "automatización de mensajes," "IA que solo responde 24/7." These re-file us into the commodity software budget.

### 3.2 Candidate hero taglines (each = employee frame + outcome before "IA")

1. «**No es un chatbot. Es un nuevo miembro de tu equipo que llena tus habitaciones.**» *(rejects the commodity category head-on)*
2. «**Te presentamos a María: la vendedora con IA que reserva, ofrece upgrades y recupera huéspedes. En WhatsApp, 24/7.**» *(the "meet Fin/Ava/Alice" named-persona pattern; 3 revenue verbs before the tech)*
3. «**Contrata al vendedor que tu hotel nunca tuvo. Atiende cada conversación mientras duermes.**» *(the "hire" verb + "mientras duermes")*
4. «**Su equipo no da abasto. Su vendedor con IA sí — 24/7, sin contratar a nadie más.**» *(the reframed core problem: human bandwidth → reservas perdidas + tickets bajos; OTAs not villainized)*
5. «**Deja de pagar por un software que responde. Contrata a un empleado que vende.**» *(the tool→worker pivot stated outright)*
6. «**Convierte cada mensaje de WhatsApp en una reserva — de día y de noche.**» *(concrete, loss-aversion-aware)*

Subhead pattern (believability): sentence 1 = what it is; sentence 2 = *why the bold claim is possible + you stay in control*. e.g. *"Responde al instante, resuelve dudas y confirma reservas por ti. Tú apruebas; un humano toma el control cuando hace falta."*

### 3.3 Own the unclaimed position

Every hotel rival leads its **headline** with *coverage/deflection* — Visito *"Every conversation, handled,"* HiJiffy *"Resolve 85% of FAQs,"* Asksuite *"digital front desk."* **None lead with a seller who produces revenue.** Our proactive verbs — **reservar, subir el valor de la estadía (upsell), recuperar huéspedes (reactivación), calificar y agendar** — are exactly what the cohort under-claims. Lead with them.

### 3.4 Voice & es-CO rules (hard)

- **Usted**, warm, respectful (Colombian business register). Warm ≠ jokey. Short sentences (Spanish runs ~15–20% longer — cut filler: "realmente," "solución integral," "de manera eficiente").
- **Jargon bans → plain outcomes:** chatbot/IA → «un empleado que atiende tu WhatsApp / tu vendedor digital»; automatizar → «responde solo, sin que muevas un dedo»; integración con tu PMS → «lee la disponibilidad de tus habitaciones en tiempo real»; plantilla/template → «mensaje»; leads → «clientes interesados»; conversión → habla en **reservas, ventas, ingresos**.
- **CTAs are verbs + outcome:** «Prueba tu agente gratis» · «Mira cómo responde a un huésped» · «Empieza a vender más». Never «Enviar / Aceptar / Solicitar demo» alone.

### 3.5 Trust grammar (for AI-skeptical, non-technical owners)

The autonomy that makes it valuable is what a wary owner distrusts. Deploy all four:
1. **Risk away with outcome language:** «se paga solo / pagas cuando trae reservas» — the single strongest trust signal.
2. **Empower, don't replace:** «tu equipo, potenciado, no reemplazado» — the agent handles the night-shift volume so your people close the VIP/group deals. (Avoid Artisan's "no sick days / stop hiring humans" — too aggressive for SMB owners who *are* the staff.)
3. **Show the work + the why:** «Porque el huésped preguntó por el spa, le ofrecí el paquete X» — our ApprovalCard house style, marketing-ized. Put a real example on the page.
4. **Human-in-the-loop always visible:** «Usted manda. Él ejecuta.» Escalation + override shown as a feature, not fine print.

## 4. Competitive landscape & our wedge

**Two tiers:**
- **Enterprise/global** (demo-led, no public price, award walls, deep integration walls): **Canary** (*"#1 Hospitality Management System,"* voice/webchat-first, 4× upsell, 80%+ automation) · **Duve** (*"Personalized hospitality at scale,"* deepest integration logo wall — ~14 PMS + smart locks + payments — 8–12% upsell conversion, ~20% revenue lift).
- **LatAm SMB** (WhatsApp-first, transparent pricing — our arena): **Visito** (`visitoai.com`) and **Alzio** (`alzio.co`).

**Alzio is the rival to beat** (Colombia, closest positioning): *"El agente de ventas IA #1 para hoteles de LATAM,"* *"Convierte cada WhatsApp de madrugada en una reserva pagada,"* 27-sec promise, native voice audio, COP pricing ($299k / $599k / $1.199M mo), 2× refund guarantee, honest/objection-forward page. **Its weaknesses we attack:** NDA-only, **unverifiable** social proof (no named hotels, no third-party badges); thin PMS list (LobbyPMS/Cloudbeds/Mews only); **no compliance story**; hype-heavy scarcity copy.

**Visito** — the polished LatAm incumbent: clean Spanish, real named boutique clients (incl. **Blue Apple Beach, Cartagena**), 100+ languages, **USD credit pricing** ("1 crédito = 1 respuesta" — metering anxiety for a small hotel), **no voice**, no PMS ownership, no compliance.

**Our uncontested white space (all three at once — nobody else can):**
1. **Own the PMS layer, don't bolt onto it.** Every rival *integrates into* a PMS; we ship a **free built-in PMS** → *"no necesitas comprar Cloudbeds aparte."* Biggest structural differentiator.
2. **DIAN + SIRE compliance.** Zero of the five mention it. *"El único agente de IA que factura ante la DIAN y te ayuda con el reporte SIRE."*
3. **Credibility over hype.** Named references + real numbers + honest tone beats Alzio's NDA-only proof and scarcity theatrics.

**Benchmarks to cite/beat (credible, sourced from the teardown):** sub-minute response · **8–12% upsell conversion**, ~**20% revenue lift** · OTA commission **15–25%** vs ~**4.5%** direct · hotels miss up to **40% of after-hours inquiries**.

## 5. Pricing narrative (protect the margin)

- **Narrative = revenue; mechanic = predictable.** Lead the *story* with outcomes/revenue (Sierra *"pay for a job well done,"* Fin *"only pay when Fin delivers value"*), bill on a **predictable plan** underneath. Data: hybrid pricing is now the most common model (25%→37% in a year); ~**80%** of buyers pick the predictable option even when pure-outcome is cheaper on paper (Decagon). Don't launch a scary "% of your bookings" bill.
- **Value metric, best→worst by willingness-to-pay:** *reservas/ingresos generados* → *clientes interesados calificados* → *conversaciones* → **mensajes (avoid as the headline unit — "makes you a commodity," Poyar).**
- **The hotel ROI anchor = the revenue your team can't physically capture** (reframed — the villain is human bandwidth, NOT the OTAs): the reservations lost after-hours / unanswered, and the tickets left low for lack of follow-up + upsell. Stack the value the AI seller recovers: (1) reservas que se perdían por no atender a tiempo, (2) tickets más altos por upsell + seguimiento, (3) reactivación de huéspedes que se enfriaron. Commission savings on direct bookings stays a *secondary* upside, never "las OTAs te roban" — OTAs are also a channel we unify.
- **Calculator, if used:** tiny and non-threatening — 3 sliders (habitaciones/mes vía OTA, tarifa promedio, % que quieres pasar a directo) → **one COP figure** + "tu plan se paga con N reservas." Name the outcome, not "Calculadora de ROI."
- **Pricing model — LOCKED (2026-08-01), numbers never public; the site shows the *model*, not the figures.** Two hotel plans, quote-only:
  - **Plan A — fijo:** higher fixed monthly fee, **0% commission** on everything.
  - **Plan B — base + comisión:** low fixed fee (covers cost) **+ Transactional reservation 1% · Upsell 5% · Reactivation 5%** (charged **only on realized/checked-out stays**; reactivation counts only when **agent-initiated**, time-agnostic). Base = realized stay value net of taxes, COP.
  - **Site treatment:** surface the *philosophy* as a trust weapon — *"Dos formas de trabajar con nosotros. En una, solo ganamos más cuando vendemos por ti."* (Sierra-style aligned incentives) — with **zero figures**. This is our answer to Alzio/Visito's public prices: same trust, more premium, no commoditization.
  - The attribution engine that makes Plan B billable + auditable is **CON-161** (separate mission-critical build).
- **De-emphasize "reemplaza a tu recepcionista."** It's soft ROI, caps WTP at one salary, and invites "then I'll just have my staff do it." Labor relief = a secondary bullet, never the headline.

## 6. Site architecture — the 3-layer pattern

Observed identically across Salesforce, HubSpot, Zendesk, Intercom, Twilio, Sierra:

**Layer A — Homepage = ONE product + breadth proof (not an industry list).** Sell the flagship (the AI seller), then prove breadth with a **specific customer count ("N+")** + a **logo wall spanning different industries**. Nobody lists industries on the hero — they *imply* "we serve everyone" through scale.

**Layer B — an "Industries/Soluciones" HUB = a grid of parallel doors.** A chooser: *"Elige tu industria"* → uniform cards (Hotelería, Comercio, Educación, "Tu sector") → a reassurance band *"Cualquier industria, cualquier tamaño."* The mere existence of several parallel deep pages **is** the credibility signal ("big enough to specialize"). HubSpot literally reuses one templated sentence across 19 industries.

**Layer C — the deep vertical subpage = the same product, re-skinned in that vertical's vocabulary.** Rename the product in the H1 ("Agentforce **for Travel & Hospitality**"), swap every noun, keep the skeleton identical across verticals.

### 6.1 Deep Hotel subpage — 10-section template (synthesized from Zendesk-retail + Salesforce-travel + Sierra-financial), mapped to our real features

1. **Hero** — outcome headline (§3.2) + subhead + 2 CTAs («Prueba tu agente gratis» / «Habla con ventas»).
2. **Same-industry proof strip** — hotel logos or a flagship + a hard stat (or a "40% of [X]" move while references are thin).
3. **Three hotel outcomes** — «Más reservas directas» · «Sube el valor de cada estadía» · «Recupera huéspedes que se enfriaron». (Maps to `create_reservation`, `get_services` upsell, reactivation.)
4. **Product modules, hotel-framed** — «Atiende y reserva por WhatsApp 24/7» (guest agent, multilingual ES/EN/PT) · «Cobra en el chat» (`create_payment_link`/Wompi) · «Tu PMS ya incluido» (built-in PMS — the moat). Each with a stat + CTA.

9. **Industry FAQ** — es-CO search language («¿Cómo responde el agente si no hay disponibilidad?», «¿Necesito cambiar mi PMS?») — doubles as SEO.
